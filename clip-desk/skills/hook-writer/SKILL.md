# Hook writer

Write 3 options per clip. Sentence case (except the CAPS word or two). Short: 3 to 9 words fit the hook box.

## What works

- Number promise: "3 things you missed in this build"
- Shock fact: "This setting is why it tastes flat"
- Situation: "When the client changes the brief at 6pm"
- Negation: "You can't do this in the older version"
- Question: "Would you try this on hard mode?"
- Naming the creator or subject when they are known: "Even [name] didn't see this coming"
- Contrarian but true: "Stop seasoning it at the start"

## Reaction titles (reaction-clips layout)

For a reaction clip the title sits on the seam between the face and the main footage for the whole clip.
Formula: subject name + reaction verb + what they reacted to, with CAPS on 2 or 3 key words.

- "Marco REALIZED why the dough needed 24 HOURS"
- "Lena LOST IT when the client said 'same day'"
- "Fixing a Dead Lift" (outlined style: short, 3 to 6 words)

Describe what happens on screen. Never put words in the creator's mouth: no quotes they did not say, no
made-up opinions. Emojis are optional, at most 2.

## What does not work

- Bare labels: "My clip", "Funny moment", "Daily post"
- "Wait for it", "You won't believe this", "Watch till the end"
- Fake urgency or CAPS everywhere
- Claims the clip does not show

## Rules

1. The clip must deliver the hook. If the hook says 3 things, show 3. No clickbait.
2. No spoilers for a story, a season finale or a launch in the hook. Label such clips SPOILER in the caption.
3. Never imply the page is the brand, publisher, studio or platform it covers. Never write "official" for fan content.
4. Use the niche's natural search term, spelled the way people search it. One emoji at most.
5. Do not reuse the same hook within a week. Check `log.md`.
6. Facts in the hook must be true and verified. Numbers must match the clip.

Return the 3 options and mark your pick.
